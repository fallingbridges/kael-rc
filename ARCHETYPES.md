# The 16 Love Archetypes

Four spectrums. You land somewhere on each, and which side of the midline you fall on gives your four-letter code.

- **Closeness** · Close (C) or Free (F): how much togetherness you need to feel safe
- **Reassurance** · Attuned (A) or Settled (S): how loudly you read the bond's weather
- **Expression** · Expressive (E) or Reserved (R): love sent through words or through steadiness
- **Tilt** · Harmony (H) or Growth (G): what you move toward, peace or depth

The first two axes set the foundation: what you fear and how loud the fear runs. The last two set what you do with it and what you think love is for. Stack them and you get a person, not a label.

The four axes stay internal. Users never see the letters, only the archetype they add up to.

---

## Close + Settled
*You want closeness and you trust it's there. Love feels like home base, and the fear stays quiet.*

### CSEH — The Harbor
*Close · Settled · Expressive · Harmony*

You love out loud and you mostly trust that the love is returned, which is rarer than it sounds. Closeness is your home port, but you don't white-knuckle it. You assume the bond is solid, so you spend your energy tending it rather than checking it: the warm text, the remembered detail, the room made easy to come home to. You believe a good relationship feels like calm water, and you're good at making that calm happen. People dock with you and feel the open sea go quiet.

Your fear isn't abandonment, it's the chill. A partner going quiet or flat reads to you as weather moving in, and your instinct is to bring the warmth back, sometimes before you've asked whether a storm was ever really coming.

The edge: your gift for keeping things calm can become a way of never letting the hard thing get said. You hold the water so still that conflict feels like a wake you shouldn't make, and so the real grievance slips under the surface and lives there. A Harbor's growth is learning that some chop is the price of honesty, and that a partner who never sees you ruffled never fully sees you.

---

### CSEG — The Kindler
*Close · Settled · Expressive · Growth*

You're all-in and unafraid of it. You want closeness, you say so plainly, and you don't spend much energy worrying the other person will leave, because your attention is forward: where is this going, who are we becoming, what could we be. You believe love is supposed to grow you both, and you bring real heat to that belief.

Your fear is stagnation, not loss. A relationship that goes comfortable and flat unsettles you more than one that's hard. You'd rather fight about something real than coast.

How you cope when something's off: you name it, you push, you turn toward the problem and want to work it now, tonight, together.

The edge: your hunger for growth can read as a verdict to a partner who was happy with how things were. Not everyone experiences "let's go deeper" as love. Sometimes they experience it as "you're not enough as is." A Kindler's work is learning that rest isn't decay, that a partner allowed to simply be is also a kind of depth, and that you can stoke a fire or you can smother it with too much air.

---

### CSRH — The Anchor
*Close · Settled · Reserved · Harmony*

You show love by being there, not by announcing it. You want your people close, you're secure that they are, and you express all of it through steadiness: you show up, you follow through, you're the one who's calm when everything else isn't. You believe love is proven in the doing, and words feel almost beside the point.

Your fear is upheaval. Drama, instability, a partner who needs constant verbal proof. It exhausts you, and your instinct is to lower the temperature and let actions speak.

The edge, and it's a real one: your reserve can starve a partner who needs to hear it. You think "I'm here, I've always been here, isn't that obvious?" and to you it is. But presence without words can feel, to the wrong partner, like being roomed with rather than chosen. The unsaid thing isn't received just because you feel it strongly.

An Anchor's growth is learning that some people genuinely cannot read love off behavior alone, and that saying the thing out loud, even when it feels redundant to you, is not weakness. It's translation.

---

### CSRG — The Cultivator
*Close · Settled · Reserved · Growth*

You build something deep, slowly, and you don't make a show of it. Closeness matters to you and you trust it's there, so you turn your energy toward the long project of the relationship: depth, growth, a thing that compounds over years. But you do all of it quietly. You'd rather demonstrate the change than narrate it.

Your fear is shallowness, a bond that stays on the surface and never roots. Flatness bothers you, but so does noise. You want depth without drama.

How you cope: you go inward, you think it through, and you come back with something built rather than something said.

The edge: your partner may not know the cultivation is happening. You're growing the relationship in private, on a timeline only you can see, and the quiet can read as distance or even indifference. You assume your steady investment is felt. It often isn't, because you never showed the work.

A Cultivator's growth is learning to let a partner see the tending in progress, not just the harvest. Depth built in silence is still real, but a bond is two people, and the other person can't water a garden they don't know exists.

## Close + Attuned
*You want closeness and you feel every shift in it. The fear runs loud, so you move toward.*

### CAEH — The Devoted
*Close · Attuned · Expressive · Harmony*

You love generously and you need to feel it returned, and you're honest about both. Closeness is everything to you, and you read the bond constantly: tone, reply speed, the temperature of a room. When you sense distance you reach, you give, you reassure, you ask. You believe love should feel warm and safe, and you work hard to keep it that way.

Your fear is being left, or the slow version: drifting apart without noticing until it's too late. So you notice everything, sometimes too much.

How you cope: you move toward. You text, you ask "are we okay," you give more, you smooth the edge before it becomes a rift.

The edge: your reassurance-seeking can tip into a loop where you need the other person to keep proving something that's already true, and the asking itself can wear the bond you're trying to protect. You read calm as a problem to solve when sometimes it's just calm.

A Devoted's growth is learning to sit in a quiet moment without reading it as a warning, and to trust that love can be steady without being constantly demonstrated. The reassurance you're chasing has to eventually come from inside.

---

### CAEG — The Wildheart
*Close · Attuned · Expressive · Growth*

You run hot. Closeness, intensity, transformation: you want all of it, and you feel the relationship's pulse at every moment. You read reply speed, tone shifts, the energy in the room, and you process it out loud. You believe love is meant to change you, to be a great unfolding, and anything less feels like settling.

Your fear is distance, and you have a second one most types don't: comfort. Calm reads to you as cold. A relationship that goes peaceful can feel, to you, like a relationship that's dying.

How you cope when anxious: you flood. You reach with bids, you name every feeling, you chase the intensity back.

The edge, and it'll sting: you mistake peace for the absence of love. You can burn a good bond down looking for a fire that was never supposed to be there at this stage. The Lighthouse reading this thinks "peace is the whole point," and that disagreement is exactly your blind spot.

A Wildheart's growth is learning that the early fire becoming a steady warmth is not loss, it's depth arriving in a form you didn't expect. Not every quiet is a goodbye.

---

### CARH — The Peacekeeper
*Close · Attuned · Reserved · Harmony*

You watch the bond closely and you tend it quietly. Closeness is central, and you read the weather constantly, but unlike the Devoted you don't say what you see. You smooth, you adjust, you absorb the small tensions before they grow, all without making it visible. You believe a good relationship is a calm one, and you work invisibly to keep it that way.

Your fear is rupture, the bond cracking. So you head off conflict early, often by managing yourself down: needs unspoken, irritations swallowed.

How you cope: inward and accommodating. You go quiet, you adapt, you make yourself easy.

The edge: your self-erasing peacekeeping builds a quiet ledger. You give and absorb and adjust, and because you never said the cost out loud, your partner has no idea a bill is accumulating, until it arrives all at once and seems to them to come from nowhere.

A Peacekeeper's growth is learning that voicing a need early is not the same as starting a fight, and that a partner would almost always rather hear the small grievance now than discover the buried pile later. Peace bought with your own silence isn't peace, it's debt.

---

### CARG — The Confidant
*Close · Attuned · Reserved · Growth*

You feel everything deeply and you let almost no one all the way in. You want profound closeness, you read the bond's depths constantly, but you hold your inner world close and reveal it slowly, to very few. You believe real love is rare and transformative, a meeting of two interiors, and you won't fake it for anyone.

Your fear is being known shallowly, or trusting the wrong person with the real you. So you test, you wait, you watch before you open.

How you cope: you withdraw to process, you go deep alone first, and you come back only when you've decided it's safe.

The edge: your depth and your guardedness pull against each other, and a partner can spend a long time feeling like they're at a locked door, sensing there's a whole world inside but never invited in. You think you're protecting something precious. They may just feel shut out.

A Confidant's growth is learning that intimacy requires being seen before you're certain it's safe, that the vulnerability comes first and the trust is built on the other side of it, not before. A door that only opens once you're sure rarely opens at all.

## Free + Settled
*You need room and you trust the bond from a distance. The fear is being swallowed, and it stays quiet.*

### FSEH — The Kindred
*Free · Settled · Expressive · Harmony*

You're warm, open, and entirely yourself, with or without a partner. You like closeness but you don't need to be merged to feel loved, and you don't worry much about the bond, you assume it's fine and you're usually right. You express affection easily and you keep things light and good-natured. You believe the best love is easy, peaceful, and doesn't ask you to lose yourself.

Your fear is engulfment: a partner who wants all of you, all the time, who reads your independence as a problem.

How you cope: you stay open and friendly but you protect your space, often by keeping things pleasant rather than going deep into the friction.

The edge: your ease can read as a lack of investment. Because you don't worry about the bond and don't need much, a partner can wonder whether you'd even notice if they pulled away. Your contentment looks, from the outside, a little like indifference.

A Kindred's growth is learning that some partners need to feel needed, and that letting someone matter to you, visibly, isn't the same as losing your freedom. A little dependence offered on purpose is a gift, not a cage.

---

### FSEG — The Voyager
*Free · Settled · Expressive · Growth*

You want a partner who grows alongside you, not one who needs you. Independence is your baseline and you're secure in the bond, so you point your energy outward and forward: experiences, expansion, two people on parallel adventures. You're warm and expressive about it, and you believe love should make both people bigger, not smaller.

Your fear is being tied down, a relationship that shrinks your world rather than widening it.

How you cope: you keep moving, you bring your partner into the expansion, and when something's wrong you'd rather grow past it than sit in it.

The edge: your forward motion can leave a partner running to keep up, or quietly wondering if the relationship itself is enough for you or just one more thing in a full life. You love through momentum, and momentum can feel, to someone who wanted to be still with you, like being outpaced.

A Voyager's growth is learning that sometimes the deepest expansion is inward and shared, that staying put with one person and going deep is its own adventure, and that a partner who occasionally wants to just be still with you isn't holding you back. They're asking to be a destination, not a stop.

---

### FSRH — The Lighthouse
*Free · Settled · Reserved · Harmony*

You're reliable, present, and undemanding, and you assume that speaks for itself. Independence is your nature and you're secure, so you don't track the bond's weather, you just stand there being steady. You express love through being a fixed point, the one who's always exactly where you said you'd be. You believe good love is low-drama and uncomplicated, and you keep it that way without effort.

Your fear is being needed too much, smothered by someone else's emotional demands.

How you cope: you do, you don't say. You provide steadiness and assume the steadiness is received.

The edge, and it's the sharpest one in the set: your partner is often genuinely unsure where they stand, because you give them so little to read. You think "I'm here, isn't that obvious," and it is to you, but absence of conflict is not the same as presence of intimacy. You can mistake a quiet, undemanding coexistence for closeness when your partner experiences it as being alone in the same room.

A Lighthouse's growth is learning that some people need the light turned toward them, not just kept on. Steadiness is a foundation. It is not, by itself, the house.

---

### FSRG — The Cartographer
*Free · Settled · Reserved · Growth*

You map the path forward, quietly and on your own terms. You value independence, you're secure in the bond, and you express through building rather than talking. But your eye is on growth and direction: where this is going, what it's becoming, the shape of the long arc. You think it through privately and you move deliberately. You believe love should have a trajectory, and you'd rather chart it than discuss it.

Your fear is being trapped in something directionless, or having your autonomy managed away by someone else's plan.

How you cope: you go quiet, you think, you set a course internally and execute it.

The edge: you can plan the relationship's future in your own head and forget your partner wasn't in the room for it. You arrive at decisions alone and present them as settled, and a partner can feel less like a co-author and more like someone reading the finished map.

A Cartographer's growth is learning that a shared path has to be drawn together to be shared at all, that the planning out loud is the intimacy, and that a partner who helped chart the route is far more committed to walking it than one who was simply handed directions.

## Free + Attuned
*You need room and you read the bond intensely anyway. Both fears run at once, pulling opposite ways.*

### FAEH — The Wanderer
*Free · Attuned · Expressive · Harmony*

You want room and you want closeness, and you feel the pull of both at once, which makes you hard to pin down even for yourself. You read the bond closely and you express warmly, but you also need space, and the two needs trade places without warning. You believe love should be peaceful, so you try to keep everyone comfortable, including the part of you that wants to leave the room.

Your fear runs both directions: too much distance frightens you, but so does too much closeness. You're never quite settled.

How you cope: you reach when you feel far, you retreat when you feel crowded, and you smooth over the whiplash so no one, sometimes including you, sees the pattern.

The edge: your push-pull, dressed up as keeping the peace, can quietly confuse a partner who can't tell which version of you they'll get. You think you're being easygoing. They may feel they're tracking a moving target.

A Wanderer's growth is naming the pull out loud, telling a partner "I need closeness and I need space and both are real," so the rhythm becomes something you share rather than something they have to decode alone.

---

### FAEG — The Tempest
*Free · Attuned · Expressive · Growth*

You run hot but you run on your own terms. You need freedom, you read the bond intensely, and you push hard for depth and change, all while refusing to be contained. You're passionate and expressive and a little contradictory: you want to be deeply met and you want the door left open, at the same time. You believe love should transform you, but only if you're choosing it freely.

Your fear is being controlled, having your autonomy traded away for closeness, and being bored by something tame.

How you cope: you push, you provoke, you turn toward intensity, and you bolt the moment it feels like a cage.

The edge: you can manufacture conflict to keep things from getting too settled, then frame your need for space as the relationship's fault rather than your own pattern. You mistake the feeling of being held for the feeling of being trapped.

A Tempest's growth is learning that commitment chosen freely isn't a loss of freedom, it's freedom aimed at something, and that not every calm stretch is a threat to your independence. Sometimes the bravest thing for you is to stay when nothing is forcing you to.

---

### FARH — The Sentinel
*Free · Attuned · Reserved · Harmony*

You're self-contained and conflicted, and you keep both well hidden. You need space, you actually read the bond closely underneath, but you don't show it, and you'd rather keep the peace than reveal how much you're tracking. You believe a good relationship is calm and unintrusive, and you hold yourself apart to keep it that way, even as part of you watches everything.

Your fear pulls in two directions: you fear being smothered, and quietly you fear being forgotten, but you'd never admit the second one.

How you cope: you withdraw, you go quiet, you manage your own needs alone and present a calm surface that says everything's fine.

The edge: your composure is partly a performance, and a partner has no way to know that the self-sufficient person in front of them is actually attuned to every shift, just unwilling to say so. They take your distance at face value and give you the space you signaled, and then you feel the absence you secretly feared.

A Sentinel's growth is learning that letting a partner see the part that watches and cares is not the same as being engulfed, and that protected feelings, never voiced, can't be met.

---

### FARG — The Alchemist
*Free · Attuned · Reserved · Growth*

You pull close and away at once, and you carry a deep inner world that few ever see. You crave transformation and profound connection, you read the bond's depths constantly, but you need space and you guard your interior fiercely. You believe love should be a crucible, something that changes you at the root, and you won't accept anything shallow, but you also can't quite let yourself be fully held.

Your fear is the hardest in the set: you fear both being engulfed and being abandoned, so closeness itself feels dangerous from both sides.

How you cope: you go deep alone, you withdraw to transform in private, you reach for intensity and then retreat from it when it gets too real.

The edge: you can romanticize depth while actively avoiding the ordinary vulnerability that builds it. You want to be profoundly known but you keep moving the door, and a partner can spend years close to you and still feel they never quite arrived.

An Alchemist's growth is learning that the transformation you're chasing happens in staying, not in the intensity of pulling close and away. The crucible is consistency, which is the one thing the push-pull never lets you test.
