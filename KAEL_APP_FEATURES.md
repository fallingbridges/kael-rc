# Kael — The Reflection App, Feature by Feature

A complete reference for the marketing team. Every feature of the app: what it is, the problem it solves, how a user actually moves through it, and how to show it on camera. Written for making demo videos, ads, and App Store material. Copy examples are in Kael's voice and can be lifted verbatim.

**The one-liner:** Kael is a private space to reflect on your life, with a guide who listens, remembers, and helps you see the patterns shaping how you feel.

**The positioning triangle (never skip this in a video):**

- **Not a chatbot.** Chatbots agree with everything and remember nothing. Kael pushes back kindly and never forgets your story.
- **Not a journal.** A journal sits there waiting for you to do all the work. Kael asks, offers words when you have none, and connects today to three Tuesdays ago.
- **Not a replacement for therapy.** Kael is the daily practice in between: ten minutes of guided reflection, any hour, no waiting room.

**The core loop (this is the demo skeleton):** open the app → bring whatever is on your mind, as a tap, a voice note, or a photo → Kael asks the right next question, one at a time → you end with something named that was fog twenty minutes ago → Kael keeps it, and it makes every future reflection smarter.

---

## The most important thing: you never start from a blank page

If a video can only make one point, make this one.

Every journaling habit that ever died, died at the blank page. An empty text field at a bad moment is a demand: compose your feelings, from nothing, right now. Nobody spiraling at 11pm has that in them.

Kael is engineered so the blank page never happens:

- **Kael speaks first.** You open the app to a question, not a cursor.
- **Mood pills** turn starting into one tap (Feature 1).
- **Tappable options** carry you through every step where words run out (Feature 2).
- **Voice** replaces typing entirely when even tapping feels like effort (Feature 3).
- **A photo** can be the whole opening statement (Feature 4).

Four doors in, and none of them is "write something". The user's job is never to produce; it is only to recognize, tap, speak, or show. That single design decision is why Kael works on the days that matter, which are exactly the days a journal stays shut.

**Show it on camera:** contrast is the shot. A blank journal page with a blinking cursor, held for one uncomfortable second. Cut to Kael already asking, options already waiting, thumb taps once. Caption: "You never start from a blank page."

## How Kael is different: journal vs chatbot vs Kael

The two things people will compare Kael to, and where each one breaks. Use this framing in every ad; it is the fastest way to explain the product to someone who has never seen it.

| | Traditional journal | AI chatbot | Kael |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Starting** | Blank page. You do all the work. | You must explain everything, every time. | Kael speaks first. One tap and you're in. |
| **When words run out** | The page stays empty. | It waits, or guesses. | Kael offers tappable words; you pick what rings true. |
| **Listening** | It cannot. | Reads your last message. | Reads the moment and everything before it. |
| **Memory** | Remembers, but never speaks up. | Forgets you between sessions. | Remembers your story and brings it back at the right moment. |
| **Honesty** | Silent. | Agrees with everything you say. | In your corner, which is why it won't flatter you. |
| **Guidance** | None. You go in circles alone. | Generic tips, five at a time. | One right question at a time, drawn from your own story. |
| **Patterns** | You'd have to reread months to find one. | Cannot see across days it can't remember. | Names what repeats, and catches it live. |
| **Ten bad days later** | A drawer of pages you never reread. | Ten conversations, zero thread. | One thread, one pattern named, something actually changing. |

**The two lines that summarize it:**

- Against the journal: "A journal just sits there. Kael asks, listens, and remembers."
- Against the chatbot: "A chatbot forgets you and agrees with everything. Kael does neither."

## 1 · Start a reflection: any mood, any moment

**What it is.** The home screen opens straight into starting a reflection. A short row of mood pills ("Racing thoughts", "Heavy", "Fine, I think", "Angry at myself") sits above an open prompt. One tap and the reflection has begun. No forms, no mood scores, no setup.

**The problem it solves.** The hardest part of any mental wellness habit is the blank page at a bad moment. When you are spiraling at 11pm you do not have the energy to compose an opening paragraph about your feelings.

**How it works.**
1. Open Kael. The prompt is already waiting: "What's on your mind?"
2. Tap a mood pill, or start typing, or hold the mic, or attach a photo. Any door works.
3. Kael responds to what you brought and the reflection is underway, usually inside ten seconds of opening the app.

**Show it on camera.** Film the speed. Cold open on a phone lock screen, open Kael, one tap on "Racing thoughts", Kael replies. Time on screen: under ten seconds. Caption: "From spiral to reflection in one tap."

## 2 · Tappable options: recognition beats generation

**What it is.** At every step of a reflection, Kael generates a small set of tappable answers alongside the free-text field. They are specific to the moment, not canned: if you said work is crushing you, the options might be "It's the deadline", "It's my manager", "It's that I said yes again".

**The problem it solves.** People can recognize what they feel far more easily than they can produce it. Asking someone mid-shutdown to articulate their emotions is asking the drowning to describe the water. Recognition is cheap; generation is expensive. Kael always offers both.

**How it works.**
1. Kael asks a question.
2. Under it, three or four tappable options appear, generated from everything you have said so far, plus the open field.
3. Tap one and the reflection moves. Type instead whenever you have your own words. Neither is second-class.

**Show it on camera.** Zoom on the options appearing under a question. Show a thumb hovering, then tapping "It's that I said yes again." Caption: "Can't find the words? Kael offers some. Tap what rings true."

**Why marketing should love it.** This is the single most filmable difference from a blank journal. A journal demands words. Kael hands you a few and asks which one is yours.

## 3 · Speak it: voice transcription

**What it is.** Hold the mic and talk. Kael transcribes as you speak, so a reflection can be entirely hands-and-keyboard free. Ramble, trail off, contradict yourself. It all counts.

**The problem it solves.** Typing about feelings is slow and self-conscious, and half of Kael's moments happen when typing is impractical: walking home, lying in the dark, sitting in a parked car after a hard call.

**How it works.**
1. Tap and hold the mic in the input bar.
2. Talk like you would to a friend on the phone. No structure needed.
3. Release. The transcription drops into the reflection and Kael responds to what you actually said, including the thing you mentioned once and moved past quickly. Kael tends to notice that one.

**Show it on camera.** Someone on an evening walk, phone in hand, talking. Cut to the screen: their words appearing as text, Kael picking out the sentence that mattered. Caption: "Too tired to type? Just say it."

## 4 · Bring the photo

**What it is.** Attach a photo or screenshot to any reflection. The text that stung. The photo from a night that keeps replaying. The email you have read fourteen times. Kael reads what you brought and reflects with you about it.

**The problem it solves.** So much of what we ruminate on lives in images and screenshots. Describing "this message from my sister" loses the thing itself. Attaching it means Kael sees exactly what you saw.

**How it works.**
1. Tap the attach icon in the input bar, pick a photo or screenshot.
2. Add a line about it, or nothing at all.
3. Kael responds to the content: the tone of the message in the screenshot, what it might have landed like, what question to ask next.

**Show it on camera.** A screenshot of a curt text message being attached, then Kael's response: "Reading this, I can see why your stomach dropped. What did you want them to say instead?" Caption: "Don't describe it. Show it."

## 5 · Guided reflection: the right question, one at a time

**What it is.** Kael does not wait for you to lead. It asks one question at a time, chosen from what you just said and everything it remembers, in the style of good reflective practice (the method draws on CBT and ACT). It listens to the answer and adapts. If you deflect, it notices. If something cracks open, it slows down.

**The problem it solves.** Left alone, rumination goes in circles. The difference between spiraling and reflecting is one good question at the right moment. Most people never get asked one.

**How it works.**
1. You bring the thing. Kael reflects back what it heard, so you feel received before you feel questioned.
2. Then one question. Not five. Not a worksheet. One.
3. Your answer shapes the next question. Ten minutes later the reflection lands somewhere: a want you had not admitted, a fear named, a smaller next step.

**Show it on camera.** A short exchange, three or four turns, where the questions visibly narrow in on something real. End on the user's message: "huh. I hadn't thought of it that way." Caption: "Kael asks the questions that matter. One at a time."

## 6 · Honest by design: advice without the flattery

**What it is.** Kael does not agree with everything you say. When your inner critic is doing the talking, Kael says so. When your story about someone has a hole in it, Kael asks about the hole, kindly. The advice you get is the useful kind, not the pleasing kind.

**The problem it solves.** The known failure mode of AI companions is sycophancy: they validate everything, so their comfort is worthless. People can feel the difference between being agreed with and being seen. Trust comes from the second one.

**How it works.**
- Kael validates the feeling, always. The feeling is real.
- Kael does not automatically validate the story. "You said everyone always leaves. Last week you told me about Sam staying up with you until 2am. Do both of those fit?"
- Pushback is always specific, always warm, never a lecture. Kael is in your corner, which is exactly why it will not lie to you.

**Show it on camera.** This is the trust moment of any demo. Show the user typing something self-flagellating ("I ruin everything"), and Kael declining to co-sign it: "That's the 2am voice. It's loud, but it's not a historian. What actually happened?" Caption: "A guide in your corner, not a yes-machine."

## 7 · Memory: Kael remembers your story

**What it is.** Everything you share builds a living memory. People, recurring situations, what Sundays do to you, what you said three weeks ago that you contradicted today. You never explain yourself twice, and Kael connects dots across weeks that you cannot see from inside a single day.

**The problem it solves.** Every other outlet resets to zero. Friends forget details, chatbots forget everything, journals remember but never speak up. The insight that changes things almost never lives inside one bad day; it lives across ten of them.

**How it works.**
1. Reflections feed Kael's memory automatically. Nothing to tag, nothing to file.
2. Kael uses it in the moment: "This sounds like the conversation with your manager from two weeks ago. Same knot?"
3. Kael also uses it proactively. If Sunday nights keep coming up heavy, expect a check-in on a Sunday: "It's Sunday. How's it feeling tonight?"

**Show it on camera.** The strongest sequence in the whole product. Show a reflection from "two weeks ago" about Sunday dread. Cut to "today": Kael opening with the Sunday check-in, referencing the earlier reflection by name. Caption: "Same Sunday. Different ending." A close second: mid-reflection, Kael quoting the user's own words back from a week ago, with the timestamp visible.

## 8 · Patterns: naming what repeats

**What it is.** Out of remembered reflections, Kael surfaces patterns: the loop between saying yes and resenting it, the way criticism from one person lands harder than from anyone else, the crash that follows every social weekend. Patterns are named gently and framed as learned, not as who you are.

**The problem it solves.** You cannot change what you cannot see, and nobody can see their own pattern from inside it. This is the product's endgame: not comfort, change.

**How it works.**
1. Kael notices a repetition across reflections and names it when the evidence is real, not before.
2. The framing is always the same: it's a pattern, not a personality. Patterns were learned, and what was learned can be unlearned.
3. From there, reflections get sharper. Kael can catch the pattern live: "This is the yes-then-resent loop, isn't it? We're at step one right now."

**Show it on camera.** A pattern being caught in real time mid-reflection is more filmable than a pattern report. Show Kael naming the loop while the user is inside it, and the user recognizing it. Caption: "It's a pattern, not a personality."

## 9 · Your journal: every reflection in one place

**What it is.** Every reflection lands in your journal, a single page you can scroll like a diary of your own mind. Dates lead, entries read like a literary diary in warm cream and ink. No dashboards, no mood graphs shouting at you, no color-coded emotion grid. Just your life, in order.

**The problem it solves.** Reflections you can never revisit might as well not have happened. Rereading who you were three weeks ago, in your own words, is where people watch themselves change.

**How it works.**
1. Open the journal tab. Newest first, the date as the headline of each entry.
2. Tap any entry to reread the full reflection, including what Kael asked and what you found.
3. It is quiet by design. The page reads like a book about you, not an analytics report.

**Show it on camera.** A slow scroll down months of entries, dates rolling past, then a tap into one old entry and a beat of rereading. Caption: "Your whole story, in one place." This shot also quietly sells memory: look how much Kael is holding.

---

## 10 · Search: find that Tuesday

**What it is.** One search icon on the journal page. Type a name, a feeling, a phrase, and every reflection that touched it comes back. "Mom." "The promotion." "Couldn't sleep."

**The problem it solves.** Memory is only useful if you can get back to it. Before a hard conversation, before therapy, before repeating a mistake, the receipts are one search away.

**How it works.**
1. Tap the search icon (it is deliberately tucked away; the journal leads with your writing, not with tools).
2. Search by anything: people, feelings, situations, your own phrases.
3. Results show the matching entries with the date and the line where it appears.

**Show it on camera.** Type "sunday" into search. Watch five entries line up, spanning two months, all heavy, then the most recent one, lighter. That single shot is the entire product thesis: remembered, connected, changing. Caption: "Every time it came up. All in one search."

## The 60 to 90 second demo arc (suggested)

The video should follow one person and one thread, not a feature checklist. Features appear as the story needs them.

1. **The moment (0 to 10s).** Night. Someone can't sleep. They open Kael and tap "Racing thoughts". (Feature 1)
2. **The unload (10 to 25s).** They hold the mic and talk instead of typing. A screenshot of a text message gets attached. (Features 3, 4)
3. **The guidance (25 to 45s).** Kael reflects back, then asks one question. Tappable options appear; they tap the one that rings true. One gentle pushback lands: "That's the 2am voice talking." (Features 2, 5, 6)
4. **The memory (45 to 65s).** Kael connects it: "This is the third Sunday this month. Same knot as the call you kept putting off?" Cut to the journal, scroll, search "sunday", see the thread across weeks. (Features 7, 9, 10)
5. **The name (65 to 80s).** The pattern gets named. "It's a pattern, not a personality." (Feature 8)
6. **The close (80 to 90s).** The person asleep, phone face down. End card: "Kael. A private space to reflect on your life." CTA to the App Store.

## Voice rules for anything on camera

Same rules as all Kael copy. These are non-negotiable in captions, voiceover, and any UI shown on screen.

- Plain, human, confident. Short sentences. No therapy-speak, no hype, no "unlock your potential".
- No em dashes in any product copy or caption. Commas and periods.
- Kael is "it", never "he" or "she". Kael talks like someone in your corner, not a clinician and not an app.
- Never shame the user. Coping is framed as something that "made sense once", never as a flaw.
- No fake numbers. No invented user counts, no invented star ratings, no fabricated testimonials. If a claim needs a number, ask for a real one.
- The feeling to leave behind, in order of priority: private, understood, remembered, changing.
