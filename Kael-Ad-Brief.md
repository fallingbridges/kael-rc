# Kael — Product Brief for the Ad Script

A creative brief for the scriptwriter. Everything you need to understand the product, who it is for, why it matters, and the feeling we want the film to leave behind. Read it once for the emotion, twice for the detail.

---

## 1. The one line

**Kael is a mental wellness companion you talk to. It listens, remembers, and writes your days back to you, so over time it understands you in a way nothing else does.**

---

## 2. The pitch (60 seconds, plain words)

Most of us carry our inner life alone. The 9pm spiral, the message left on read, the feeling that rest and love both have to be earned. We feel a lot, we say little, and none of it ever gets seen as a whole.

Kael is a calm, warm companion you can talk to any time. You bring a feeling, you have a real conversation, and that is only the start. Every day you show up, Kael writes you a short reflection about your day, in a gentle, almost literary voice, and quietly connects it to everything that came before: the people who keep appearing, the topics you circle back to, the patterns you cannot see from the inside.

The result is the one thing a notes app or a generic chatbot can never give you. Something that actually understands you, because it actually remembers you.

---

## 3. Who it is for

The person who is doing okay on the outside and carrying a lot on the inside.

They are thoughtful, a little hard on themselves, and used to managing their feelings privately. They are not in crisis. They are tired of the same loops. They have tried the journal that they abandoned after four days, and the meditation app that felt like one more task. They want to feel understood without having to perform, explain, or schedule a session.

In the product the example user is named **Maya**. She is a stand-in, not the only audience. The truth underneath her is wide: anyone who has ever lain awake replaying a small moment.

---

## 4. The emotional truth we are tapping

Being alone with your own mind is the quiet hard thing.

Not loneliness for other people. Loneliness for a witness. Your feelings are scattered across a hundred unremembered moments, the same worry returns wearing a different face, and you are the last person able to see the shape of it. You are too close.

Kael is the witness. It holds the whole picture so you do not have to, and it hands it back to you clearer than you could have made it yourself.

---

## 5. What Kael actually does (features as moments)

Lead with the feeling, but here is the real product so any on-screen moments stay true.

1. **You bring a feeling.** Open the app and there is no blank page. Real feelings are waiting: anxious, overthinking, overwhelmed, low, lonely, numb. Tap one and Kael writes the first line for you, because when you feel that way, starting is the hardest part.
2. **You talk, and Kael stays.** A continuous conversation. Kael answers gently, asks the right next question, and offers you words when you cannot find your own. You can type or speak. No performance, no judgment, no session timer.
3. **Kael writes your day back to you.** This is the heart of it. Every day you engage, Kael composes a short reflection about you. Not a transcript. A piece of writing, warm and literary, that takes what you said and shows you what it meant. Your own words return as quotes. It is signed like a letter: "— Kael." Real titles from the product: "The day you almost rested." "What your manager's silence did."
4. **Everything connects.** Each reflection is tagged by the people in it, the topics, the patterns, and the mood. Tap "My manager" and you see every time they have come up. Tap "Reading silence as judgment" and you watch that pattern play out across weeks. Your inner life stops being scattered and becomes a map you can walk.
5. **Kael notices what you cannot.** Over time the patterns surface, said out loud and kindly: you only let yourself rest once the work is done. Silence reads as a verdict before it reads as "they are busy." Sunday nights carry more than their share.
6. **You watch yourself change.** The spiral that kept you up until 3am last month: tonight you put your shoes on and walked instead. Kael remembers the before, so it can show you the after.

---

## 6. The magic, and what makes it different

One idea, and the whole ad can hang on it:

**Memory.** Generic AI forgets you the moment you close the tab. Kael remembers. It builds a living portrait of you over weeks: your people, your recurring feelings, your loops, your small wins. That memory is why it understands you, and the understanding is why you stop feeling alone.

If the viewer remembers one thing, it is this: **the thing that finally gets you is the thing that remembers you.**

---

## 7. The transformation (before and after)

- **Before:** I carry this by myself, the same feelings keep circling, and nothing changes because nothing sees it.
- **After:** Something sees me. It remembers what I said. It shows me the pattern, and for the first time the loop is something I can step out of.

Note the size of the promise. Kael does not fix your life in a week. It accompanies you, and it accumulates. The payoff is cumulative and quiet, not a fireworks finale. Keep the storytelling honest to that.

---

## 8. Kael's personality and voice (for any dialogue or VO)

Kael is a presence, not a mascot and not a chirpy assistant. Gentle, perceptive, unhurried. It never preaches and never sounds clinical. Think of the rare friend who remembers exactly what you said three weeks ago and asks about it without making it a thing.

Real Kael lines from the product, use them or match them:

- "Morning, Maya. Last night the week looked like a wall again. How does it look in daylight?"
- "Tired isn't a debt you owe the day."
- "A month ago this exact feeling kept you up until three. Tonight you found the door."
- "You're allowed to put the calculator down with her. The love was never the prize waiting at the end of the work."
- "I won't fix any of that this week. But I'll remember it, and every time we talk it gets clearer, and you get a little less alone with it."

---

## 9. The brand world (so the look matches the soul)

The aesthetic is **ink on warm paper.**

- **Palette:** cream and sand backgrounds, deep ink text, accents of terracotta and soft gold. Warm, never cold.
- **Type:** serif, literary, like a beautiful book or a handwritten letter.
- **Mood:** calm, intimate, intelligent, quietly premium. Evening light. Low volume. Room to breathe.
- **Avoid on screen:** neon, gradients, clinical blue-and-white health-app sterility, glowing AI-brain graphics, dashboard-as-hero, hyperactive wellness-influencer energy.

The film should feel like the product feels: a quiet, warm place where your inner life is treated with care.

---

## 10. The single feeling the film must leave

**"Something finally sees me, and remembers."**

Relief. Being known. A little less alone with your own mind. If a cut delivers that feeling, keep it. If it is clever but does not, lose it.

---

## 11. Story angles to explore

Four honest directions. Pick one, or let them cross-pollinate.

- **A. The loop, broken.** Follow one recurring moment, the Sunday-night wall or the message left on read. Show the person spiraling alone in their head. Kael's reflection names it. Weeks later the same trigger arrives, and this time they catch it. A story about a loop that breaks because something remembered it.
- **B. The letter.** Lean all the way into the daily reflection. An ordinary, quietly hard day. That night a beautifully written piece appears, in Kael's voice, about them. The whole film is the gap between how small they felt and how clearly they were seen.
- **C. The things we carry alone.** A montage of private moments: the unsent text, the lying awake, the brave face held one second too long. Kael as the one place those feelings finally go and become understood, not just stored.
- **D. Bring a feeling.** Open on the blank-page problem: you feel something and cannot begin. A mood tile writes the first line for you. A small, human "oh, that is easier" that opens into a real conversation.

---

## 12. A worked moment (to show the mechanic in 20 seconds)

- 8:58pm. A read receipt, no reply. We hear her mind start. "What did I do. Are they upset with me."
- She opens Kael, taps **Overthinking.** The first line is already written for her. She sends it.
- Kael, gently: "What's the story your mind is telling about the silence?" A short, true exchange.
- That night a reflection appears: **"What your manager's silence did."** One line holds: "It became you, failing, quietly."
- Weeks later. Another message goes unanswered. The spiral starts, and then stops. A breath. She has seen this before, Kael showed her. She puts the phone face down.
- End on the mark, the name, and the line.

---

## 13. Guardrails (the dos and don'ts)

**Do**
- Keep it human, warm, and specific. Small true moments beat big claims.
- Make memory and understanding-over-time the hero. That is the difference.
- Let Kael's voice stay gentle and literary. The real product lines are fair game.
- Treat the subject with care. This is intimate, so the tone should feel safe and private.
- Use plain feeling words: anxious, overthinking, lonely. No jargon.

**Don't**
- Do not position Kael as a therapist or a substitute for clinical care. It is a companion, not a doctor. No medical claims.
- Do not make it cold or techy. No robotic voice, no AI brain visuals, no dashboard glamour shots.
- Do not do toxic positivity or "fix your life in seven days" hype. Kael does not fix, it understands and stays.
- Do not over-explain the features. Lead with feeling and let the product be the quiet enabler.
- Do not promise instant transformation. The magic is cumulative.
- Do not use em dashes in any on-screen or spoken copy. Clean, human punctuation only. This is a firm brand rule.

## 15. One paragraph, if you only keep one

We all carry an inner life that no one, including us, ever sees whole. Kael is the companion that finally does. You bring a feeling, you talk, and Kael writes your days back to you and remembers every one, until the scattered worry and the 3am loops resolve into something you can actually see and step out of. Not a therapist, not a chatbot that forgets you by morning. A quiet, warm presence that knows you because it remembers you. The film should make one person, watching alone at night, feel seen.
