# Kael Onboarding V7 — Screens, Copy & Psychology

A screen-by-screen reference for the V7 onboarding funnel. Every line of on-screen copy is quoted verbatim, followed by what the screen is doing psychologically and what job it has in the funnel. No technical detail; this is for working on copy and structure.

**The shape of the flow:** 47 screens in four acts.

1. **Act 1 — The pitch and the intake.** Who Kael is, what the product does, why it's safe, and who you are. Ends with a promise: answer honestly and Kael will show you your pattern.
2. **Act 2 — The quiz.** 16 questions across four themed blocks (mind, energy, inner voice, coping), with a "breather" story beat between each block so it never feels like a form.
3. **Act 3 — The mirror.** The payoff: your named pattern (1 of 16) and a long personalized read that echoes the user's own answers back at them.
4. **Act 4 — The close.** Confirmation that the coach is calibrated, the before/after, a signed self-commitment, a celebration, and the 30-day journey. Then the paywall (served by Superwall, outside this flow).

**The one-sentence psychology of the whole funnel:** the user pays for the quiz with attention, gets paid back with self-recognition, and by the time the price appears they have already named their problem, signed a promise to work on it, and seen the 30 days it buys.

---

## Copy voice rules (how all of this is written)

- Plain, human, confident. Short sentences. No jargon, no therapy-speak, no hype.
- No em dashes anywhere in product copy. Commas and periods only.
- One idea per screen. If a screen needs a paragraph to explain itself, it's wrong.
- Options always sit directly under the question.
- Never more than 4 questions between breaks; every break earns its place by telling part of the product story.
- Kael is "it," never "he." Kael talks like someone in your corner, not a clinician and not an app.
- Nothing shames the user. Coping mechanisms are framed as things that "made sense once," never as flaws.

# ACT 1 — The pitch and the intake

The job of Act 1: make the user feel safe, understood, and curious enough to give 16 honest answers. The product story is told up front in four beats (who Kael is, when it's there, where your words live, what changes) before we ask for anything.

### 1 · Intro, beat 1 — "Who this is"

A breathing orb animates, then Kael's first message types itself out letter by letter, like a real chat.

- **Title:** "Your pocket mental wellness coach."
- **Kael's typed message:** "Hey. I'm Kael. Someone in your corner, any hour. No judgment, no waiting room."
- **Contrast line below:** "Not a chatbot. Not a journal that sits there. **A coach who pays attention.**" (bold on the last sentence)
- **CTA:** Continue

**Psychology:** first impressions do two jobs at once: warmth (the typed message reads as a person arriving) and category separation. "No judgment, no waiting room" positions against therapy's friction; the contrast line positions against ChatGPT and journaling apps in one breath. "Pays attention" plants the memory/insight promise that beat 4 pays off.

### 2 · Intro, beat 2 — "When it's there"

A grid of six mood pills above a chat input. Tapping a mood fills the input with that feeling in first-person words, e.g. tapping "Spiraling" fills in "I can't stop spiraling."

- **Title:** "Any mood. / Any moment. / Kael *meets* you there." (three lines, "meets" italic)
- **Mood pills:** Spiraling · Anxious · Burnt out · Feeling low · Numb · Can't sleep
- **Input placeholder:** "Tell Kael anything…" (with a microphone icon)
- **Note below:** "Any hour. Type it, or just say it out loud."
- **CTA:** Continue

**Psychology:** recognition. Six moods cover nearly everyone's bad day; the user silently self-identifies with at least one. The tap-to-fill interaction is a tiny product demo: it shows Kael lowering the "I don't know what to say" barrier, which is the number one blocker to opening any wellness app. The mic note removes the effort objection.

### 3 · Intro, beat 3 — "Where your words live"

An honest imitation of the product: a soft panel labeled like the user's own library, with three reflection cards cascading in.

- **Title:** "Your safe space to / *reflect* on your life." (two lines, "reflect" italic)
- **Subtitle:** "Big feelings, small thoughts, hard days, quiet wins. It all belongs here."
- **Panel label:** "Your reflections"
- **The three reflection cards:** "The call I keep putting off" · "Sunday nights feel heavy" · "Why do I push people away?"
- **Coda:** "Every reflection helps Kael understand you better."
- **CTA:** Continue

**Psychology:** this screen sells the container, not the coach. The three card titles are deliberately specific and ordinary (avoidance, dread, a relationship question) so the user projects their own version of each. The coda is the compounding hook: your words are not just stored, they make Kael smarter about you. It bridges directly into beat 4.

### 4 · Intro, beat 4 — "What changes"

A dated chat thread plays out message by message: a Sunday night, weeks into using Kael.

- **Title:** "Break the *patterns* keeping you stuck." ("patterns" italic)
- **Subtitle:** "Kael remembers. Connects patterns. Offers insights that deepen over time."
- **Chat thread, labeled "Sunday · 9:14 PM":**
  - Kael: "It's Sunday. You said these nights used to feel impossible."
  - User: "Felt it coming. Went for a walk instead of spiraling."
  - Kael: "A month ago this was 3am, wide awake. Look at you."
- **Note below:** "Same Sunday. Different ending."
- **CTA:** Let's begin

**Psychology:** the outcome, dramatized. Instead of claiming "Kael helps you change," the user watches a future version of themselves handle a Sunday night differently, with Kael citing their own history ("You said these nights used to feel impossible"). This is the memory promise from beat 1, shown working. "Let's begin" turns the reader into a participant.

### 5 · What brings you here?

The first real question. A list of seven options; picking one shows a warm one-line acknowledgment right under the choice.

- **Title:** "What brings you here?"
- **Subtitle:** "Pick what's closest. We start there."
- **Options:** Overthinking everything · Stress and burnout · Anxiety · Being hard on myself · Feeling low · A habit I want to change · Something else
- **Acknowledgment lines (shown for the picked option):**
  - Overthinking everything → "Spirals feel like thinking. Usually they're feeling, looking for somewhere to land."
  - Stress and burnout → "You're carrying a lot. Let's look at what's actually draining you."
  - Anxiety → "That low hum of dread. You're in the right place for it."
  - Being hard on myself → "The inner critic is louder than it should be. We can turn it down."
  - Feeling low → "When everything feels heavy. We'll take this gently."
  - A habit I want to change → "It takes something just to name it. No judgment here."
- **CTA:** Continue

Tapping "Something else" skips the acknowledgment and jumps straight to the free-text screen below.

**Psychology:** the intake question doubles as the first moment of being heard. The acknowledgment line is the whole trick: the user says something vulnerable and the product answers like a person, immediately. Each reply also quietly reframes the problem in Kael's language (spirals are feelings, the critic can be turned down). The user's pick seeds their result and gets echoed back later in their read.

### 6 · Say it in your words (conditional)

Only appears if the user picked "Something else." A single short text field.

- **Title:** "Say it in your words."
- **Subtitle:** "Whatever is on your mind right now. Keep it short."
- **Placeholder:** "In a few words…" (50 character limit)
- **CTA:** Continue

**Psychology:** the escape hatch that makes the list above feel complete. Whatever they type here gets woven verbatim into their personalized read later, which is the strongest personalization moment in the funnel for the users who take it.

### 7 · The promise

A full-screen typographic statement, no imagery.

- **Kicker:** "Now, a promise"
- **Title:** "There's a logic to *how you feel*." ("how you feel" italic)
- **Subtitle:** "A few honest minutes, and I'll show you how you handle stress, what sets you off, and the pattern underneath it."
- **CTA:** Show me

**Psychology:** this is the deal being struck. "There's a logic to how you feel" is the core belief the entire funnel sells: your mess is not random, it has a shape, and a shape can be worked with. The subtitle names the exact payoff (how you handle stress, what sets you off, the pattern underneath) so the quiz ahead has a purpose. "Show me" makes the user demand the quiz rather than endure it.

### 8 · Privacy

A shield badge and a short trust statement.

- **Kicker:** "Just between us"
- **Title:** "Private, secure, / and yours alone."
- **Subtitle:** "No one reads your world but you. Which means you can be honest here, even about the messy parts."
- **CTA:** I value my privacy

**Psychology:** placed right before we ask for a name and honest answers, because honesty is the quiz's raw material. The framing is functional, not legal: privacy is sold as the thing that lets you be honest, not as a compliance checkbox. The CTA is an identity statement the user affirms about themselves, which also pre-commits them to answering honestly.

### 9 · Name

- **Title:** "What should Kael call you?"
- **Subtitle:** "So it can talk to you like a person, not a user."
- **Placeholder:** "Your first name"
- **CTA:** Continue

**Psychology:** the first personal token. The subtitle gives the reason in the product's own voice, so the ask feels like relationship-building, not data collection. The name comes back at the promise screen ("Maya, let's make a promise.") and in the age question, small proofs that Kael listens.

### 10 · Age

**Psychology:** immediately uses the name they just gave. Every demographic ask carries a "this makes your read better" justification, keeping the frame on their upcoming result rather than on us collecting data.

- **Options:** Woman · Man · Non-binary · Prefer not to say

**Psychology:** same pattern as age: the justification is personalization ("not a generic template"), and "Prefer not to say" keeps it pressure-free.

### 12 · What's weighing most

Tap a card; auto-advances.

- **Subtitle:** "So Kael knows where to start."
- **Options:** Work and pressure · Relationships · Health and energy · Just feeling off · A bit of everything · Not sure yet

**Psychology:** a low-effort life-domain question that keeps the "getting to know you" rhythm going. "Just feeling off," "A bit of everything," and "Not sure yet" mean nobody stalls here; there is always an honest option.

### 13 · Quiz doorstep

- **Subtitle:** "A 3-minute quiz on how you feel, cope, talk to yourself, and recover."

**Psychology:** sets the contract for Act 2: what it is (a quiz), how long (3 minutes), what it covers, and what's at stake (your pattern, one of 16). The "16 patterns" kicker creates the curiosity gap: which one am I?

### 14 · Credibility breather

**Psychology:** the science beat, placed at the doorstep of the quiz so the questions ahead read as an instrument, not a BuzzFeed quiz. Each method gets a five-word plain-English translation so the acronyms build trust instead of alienating.

# ACT 2 — The quiz

Sixteen questions in four themed blocks of four: **Your mind → Your energy → Your inner voice → How you cope.** The current block name is always visible as an eyebrow above a four-segment progress bar, so the user always knows where they are and how much is left. Question formats rotate (two big cards, sliders, agree/disagree statements, multi-selects) so it never feels repetitive. Card questions advance themselves on tap.

A note on sliders: every slider starts at the center and carries a permission line ("Slide toward whichever fits. There's no wrong spot." on sliders, "Drag to wherever you land. No wrong answer." on statements). Honesty over performance, always.

### Block 1 · Your mind

**15 ·** Two cards: "Be honest. How's your head most days?" → "Mostly busy and hard to quiet" / "Mostly quiet and clear"

**16 ·** Slider: "How often do you replay a conversation after it's over?" → Rarely ↔ Almost always

**17 ·** Statement, rated on a slider under "Does this sound like you?": *"My brain won't switch off when I'm trying to sleep."* → Not me ↔ Exactly me

**18 ·** Slider: "When something's uncertain, how often does your mind jump to the worst case?" → Rarely ↔ Almost always

**Psychology of the block:** opens with the most relatable symptom cluster (racing thoughts) so the first answers come easy. "Be honest" in the very first prompt sets the tone for all sixteen. The statement format works because it sounds like something the user has literally said to themselves.

### 19 · Breather 1 (after the mind block)

- **Kicker:** "Already, a shape"
- **Title:** "There's a pattern in how your mind runs."
- **Body:** "Not better, not worse. Just yours. Kael learns it so it can catch the spiral *the moment it starts*."
- **CTA:** Continue

**Psychology:** early proof of progress. Four questions in, the product claims it can already see something. "Not better, not worse. Just yours" defuses test anxiety (this is not a grade), and the body restates the product promise mid-quiz.

### Block 2 · Your energy

**20 ·** Two cards: "By the end of most days, you're" → "Running on empty" / "Still got something left"

**21 ·** Slider: "How often do you feel drained before the day's even begun?" → Rarely ↔ Almost always

**22 ·** Statement: *"I push through until I crash."* → Not me ↔ Exactly me

**23 ·** Multi-select: "Where does stress show up in your body?" with subtitle "Pick up to three. Your body keeps the score." → Tight chest · Restless sleep · Jaw tension · Shallow breathing · Low energy · A knot in my stomach. The button reads "None of these" until something is picked, then "Continue."

**Psychology of the block:** burnout territory. The body question is the block's personalization engine: whatever the user picks here gets quoted back to them word for word in their read ("Your body's been keeping score: tight chest and restless sleep."). "None of these" as the untouched CTA means skipping is a real answer, never a failure.

### 24 · Breather 2 — the live chat demo

- **Kicker:** "Why Kael, and not a chatbot"
- **Title:** "Kael hands you a way forward."
- **Body:** "A blank chatbot waits for you to find the words. Kael offers *a few to tap* instead."
- **Live demo beneath:** a Kael message reading "That sounds like a lot to hold on your own. Want to start with the part that's loudest right now?" with three tappable reply chips: "The racing thoughts" · "Why I can't rest" · "I just need to vent" and an input hinting "or type your own…"
- **CTA:** Continue

**Psychology:** the sharpest differentiation moment in the funnel, and it's shown, not told. The user sees the actual interaction pattern that separates Kael from ChatGPT: you are never staring at a blank box. Placing a mini product demo mid-quiz also breaks the question rhythm exactly when fatigue would start.

### Block 3 · Your inner voice

**25 ·** Two cards: "The voice in your head is usually" → "A tough critic" / "On your side"

**26 ·** Slider: "How often do you feel like you're not quite enough?" → Rarely ↔ Almost always

**27 ·** Statement: *"I'm harder on myself than I'd ever be on a friend."* → Not me ↔ Exactly me

**28 ·** Slider: "How often does one small slip stick with you for days?" → Rarely ↔ Almost always

**Psychology of the block:** the self-criticism block goes third, once trust is built, because these questions cut closest. Question 27 is the emotional center of the whole quiz; nearly everyone who needs this product answers "Exactly me," and answering it is itself a small confession.

### 29 · Breather 3 (after the inner voice block)

- **Kicker:** "The honest part"
- **Title:** "That voice isn't telling the truth."
- **Body:** "It's just loud, and it's had years of practice. Kael helps you hear it as *a voice, not a verdict*."
- **CTA:** Continue

**Psychology:** the user just spent four questions with their inner critic; this beat takes their side against it. It is the first taste of what Kael's coaching actually sounds like, delivered at the exact moment it's relevant. "A voice, not a verdict" is a keeper phrase that recurs in the reads.

### Block 4 · How you cope

**30 ·** Two cards: "When a feeling gets heavy, you" → "Reach for something to take the edge off" / "Sit with it and let it pass"

**31 ·** Slider: "How often do you numb out instead of feeling it?" → Rarely ↔ Almost always

**32 ·** Statement: *"I can sit with discomfort without needing to fix it right away."* → Not me ↔ Exactly me

**33 ·** Multi-select: "When it gets heavy, what do you reach for?" with subtitle "Pick any that fit, or none at all. No judgment, Kael's seen them all." → Endless scrolling · A drink or a smoke · Burying it in work · Zoning out · Keeping constantly busy · Shutting people out. Optional; "None of these" until picked.

**Psychology of the block:** the most confessional block goes last, when investment is highest. The subtitle on 33 does heavy lifting: "No judgment, Kael's seen them all" gives permission to admit the drink, the scroll, the shutdown. These picks also feed the read verbatim ("when it rises, you reach for a drink and the endless scroll"), which is the moment users describe as "scarily accurate."

### 34 · Breather 4 (after the coping block)

- **Kicker:** "What it adds up to"
- **Title:** "However you cope, it made sense once."
- **Body:** "Nothing here is a flaw. It's how you learned to get through. From here, Kael helps you *keep the relief* and drop the cost."
- **CTA:** Continue

**Psychology:** absolution before the reveal. The user has just admitted their numbing behaviors; this beat immediately reframes them as learned survival, not weakness. "Keep the relief and drop the cost" is the product's theory of change in eight words: Kael doesn't take your coping away, it upgrades it.

### 35 · Goals

Multi-select list.

- **Title:** "What do you want to get out of this journey?"
- **Subtitle:** "Pick as many as feel true. Kael points everything toward them."
- **Options:** A calmer mind · Breaking the cycle · Being kinder to myself · Steadier energy · Feeling like myself again · Just some relief
- **CTA:** Continue

**Psychology:** the pivot from problems to hope. After sixteen questions about what's wrong, the user names what they want instead. Each option is an emotional outcome, not a feature. "Feeling like myself again" is the quiet best-performer of the set; it names the loss underneath everything else.

### 36 · What's been showing up

Multi-select list.

- **Title:** "What's been showing up lately?"
- **Subtitle:** "Choose all that apply."
- **Options:** Racing thoughts · Overthinking · Stress · Low energy · Feeling low · Trouble sleeping · Irritability · Mostly just curious
- **CTA:** Continue

**Psychology:** the symptom inventory. Ticking off concrete symptoms crystallizes the need the plan is about to answer, and a longer list of picks makes the upcoming calibration feel like it has more to work with. "Mostly just curious" is the out for browsers, so no one has to lie.

### 37 · The therapist question

Yes/No cards; auto-advances.

- **Title:** "Did you hear about Kael from a therapist?"
- **Subtitle:** "Either way, you're in the right place."

**Psychology:** whichever way it's answered, the question itself implants that therapists recommend Kael. The subtitle catches both answers warmly. One tap, high trust yield.

### 38 · Daily goal

Four tiers, each pairing minutes with the outcome those minutes buy. Tap to pick; auto-advances.

- **Title:** "Set your daily goal."
- **Subtitle:** "A few honest minutes is enough."
- **Tiers:** 3 min/day "A gentle start" · 5 min/day "Build the habit" · 10 min/day "Go deeper" · 15+ min/day "Shift the pattern"

**Psychology:** a micro-commitment. Choosing a number converts the user from evaluator to participant, and the outcome pairing ("Shift the pattern") sells the bigger tiers without pressure. The subtitle keeps the floor low so the commitment feels winnable.

### 39 · Notifications

- **Kicker:** "One small thing"
- **Title:** "Want Kael to check in gently?"
- **Subtitle:** "A quiet nudge when it helps, nothing more."
- **CTA:** Yes, check in on me · quiet secondary: Not now

**Psychology:** the notification ask, reframed as care ("check in gently") rather than engagement. Asked after the daily-goal commitment, so saying yes is just being consistent with the goal they set ten seconds ago. "Not now" is visually quiet but real.

### 40 · Calibration

A full-screen percentage counts from 0 to 100 over about seven seconds, with four progress rows filling one after another and rotating five-star review quotes underneath. Advances by itself.

- **Progress steps:** "Reading your answers" → "Mapping how you cope" → "Weighing what drains you" → "Finding the thread underneath"
- **Rotating reviews:** "Felt like it actually knew me." · "I finally have words for it." · "Scarily accurate, in the best way." · "The first one that didn't feel generic." (all five stars)

**Psychology:** the labor illusion. A deliberate pause that makes the analysis feel earned and substantial; instant results feel cheap. The step labels narrate real-sounding work built from the user's actual inputs, and the reviews prime how to feel about the read that's coming ("scarily accurate") right before it arrives.

# ACT 3 — The mirror

The payoff of the whole funnel. Everything until now bought this moment.

### 41 · The reveal

A quiet, ceremonial screen: a small label, a large glyph, the pattern name, one line.

- **Label:** "What Kael noticed"
- **Then:** the pattern's glyph, its name (e.g. "The Perfectionist"), and its one-line essence (e.g. "A sharp mind and a sharper inner critic.")
- **CTA:** Continue

**Psychology:** the identity moment. It's framed as an observation ("What Kael noticed"), not a diagnosis or a personality badge, which keeps it humble and believable. One of 16 named patterns; the full list with essences is in the appendix. Being named is inherently shareable and inherently sticky; users will refer to themselves as "a Brooder" the way they say their star sign.

### 42 · The read

A long-scroll personalized document, signed by Kael. Structure:

- **Eyebrow:** "Your pattern" · the pattern name · a 2-3 sentence opening written for that exact combination of traits
- **"Your four pillars":** four level meters showing where the user landed on each axis: Your mind (Quiet ↔ Racing) · Your energy (Steady ↔ Running low) · Inner voice (Kind ↔ Critical) · How you cope (Faces it ↔ Numbs it)
- **Four titled sections, driven by the user's loudest pillar:** "How you carry it" · "What you're after" · "What sets it off" · "How you cope." Each section has a short body written in second person, a row of recognition chips (e.g. "replays it after," "three thoughts deep"), and a "how Kael helps" note marked with a spark.
- **The bodies echo the user's own answers back:** the thing they said brought them here, where they said stress lands in their body, and what they said they reach for, all quoted in prose.
- **A one-line accent** acknowledging their second-loudest pillar (e.g. "And the inner critic is loud too, quicker to your faults than your wins.")
- **"Where this goes":** three forward-looking lines on what changes with Kael.
- **Sign-off:** "— Kael" with the note "This read sharpens the more you talk to me."
- **CTA:** This sounds like me

**Psychology:** this is the "scarily accurate" moment the calibration reviews promised. Three mechanisms make it land: specificity (their own words quoted back), structure (it reads like a document someone wrote about you, not a horoscope), and balance (each section pairs the pain with how Kael helps, so the read sells the product while it validates the person). The CTA is the conversion linchpin: tapping "This sounds like me" is the user affirming the diagnosis, and everything after it builds on that agreement.

# ACT 4 — The close

From here to the paywall, every screen deepens commitment: the receipt, the contrast, the signed promise, the celebration, the journey, then the price.

### 43 · Your coach is ready

- **Glyph** (the user's pattern icon) · **Kicker:** "Calibrated to you"
- **Title:** "Your coach is ready."
- **Checklist:** ✓ Tuned to your pattern · ✓ Pointed at your goals · ✓ Fit to your daily rhythm · ✓ Here any hour you need it
- **CTA:** Continue

**Psychology:** the receipt. Everything the user gave (quiz answers, goals, daily minutes) is read back as a completed setup, converting their effort into a finished object that now exists and is theirs. Loss aversion starts here: walking away now means abandoning something already built for you.

### 44 · The difference

Two cards side by side: "Now," small and low; "With Kael," larger and raised.

- **Kicker:** "The difference"
- **Title:** "See the difference / *with Kael.*"
- **Now (marked with ✕):** Racing thoughts · Heavy mornings · Harsh self-talk · Bottled up
- **With Kael (marked with ✓):** A settled mind · Steadier days · A kinder voice · Room to feel
- **Badge on the With Kael card:** "A calmer mind"
- **Note below:** "Same days. A different way through."
- **CTA:** I want that

**Psychology:** the classic before/after, kept honest. Each "Now" line maps one-to-one to a "With Kael" line, and the note manages expectations beautifully: your life doesn't change, your way through it does. The CTA converts the comparison into a stated desire; the user literally says "I want that" before seeing a price.

### 45 · The promise

A contract-like card with the user's name in the title, three vows, Kael's counter-promise, and a real signature box the user draws in with their finger. The CTA stays disabled until they sign.

- **Title:** "{Name}, let's make *a promise.*"
- **Lead line:** "From today, I promise to"
- **The vows:** ✓ Be honest about what I'm feeling · ✓ Face the hard stuff instead of burying it · ✓ Be kinder to myself along the way
- **Kael's side:** "**And Kael promises back.** *To remember, to notice, to be there at 2am.*"
- **Signature box hint:** "Sign with your finger" (captioned with the user's name)
- **Note below:** "Your signature stays on this screen. Nothing is saved."
- **CTA:** I commit to myself

**Psychology:** the strongest commitment device in the funnel. The vows are deliberately about the inner work, not about using the product, so the user is committing to themselves, with Kael as witness. The physical act of signing engages effort and ownership in a way a button never can, and "To remember, to notice, to be there at 2am" makes it mutual. The privacy note keeps the ritual safe. The CTA says what the signature meant.

### 46 · You're all set!

A celebration that plays itself: a seal stamps in with a check, a gold bloom, a one-time burst of confetti, rings rippling out, and then the title streams in letter by letter. No button; it advances on its own after the moment lands.

- **Title:** "You're all set!"

**Psychology:** the ceremony. The user just signed something; this is the funnel honoring it. It marks the completion of onboarding (not the promise) and creates a peak-emotion beat right before the commercial ask, so the paywall arrives on a high rather than after a wall of screens.

### 47 · Your 30 days with Kael

A milestone timeline on a thick vertical rail. The "Today" node is already filled in dark (the journey has started); days 3, 7, and 30 sit on the track ahead.

- **Title:** "Your 30 days with Kael."
- **Today: Bring what's on your mind** — "Start anywhere. Kael starts learning."
- **Day 3: Your loops get caught early** — "Kael spots them before you finish naming them."
- **Day 7: Your first shift, named** — "One spiral caught, one kinder word to yourself."
- **Day 30: The pattern loosens its grip** — "The old reflex shows up. You catch it, and choose."
- **CTA:** Continue → the paywall

**Psychology:** the last screen before the price gives the price an object: not "a subscription," but these 30 days. Every milestone is a transformation beat, not a billing event, which keeps this screen offer-agnostic (the actual offer, trial or otherwise, is served by Superwall and can change without touching this screen). "Today" already filled says the journey has begun; paying is continuing, not starting. The milestones are believable on purpose: day 30 is not a cure, it's "you catch it, and choose."

### After 47 · The paywall

Served by Superwall, outside this flow. Offers, trials, and pricing vary by campaign, which is exactly why screens 44 to 47 never mention terms.

# Appendix

## The four pillars

Every user is scored on four axes. Each resolves to one of two poles, and the four together produce a 4-letter code, which maps to 1 of 16 patterns.

| Pillar | Question it answers | Poles |
|---|---|---|
| Your mind | Does it race or rest? | Quiet ↔ Racing |
| Your energy | Full tank or running on fumes? | Steady ↔ Running low |
| Inner voice | Ally or critic? | Kind ↔ Critical |
| How you cope | Feel it or mute it? | Faces it ↔ Numbs it |

## The 16 patterns

Each has a bespoke name, essence line, and opening paragraph. Names and essences:

| Pattern | Essence |
|---|---|
| The Overloaded | Everything's loud at once, and you're running on fumes. |
| The Striver | Racing, drained, self-critical, and pushing through it all. |

## Personalization tokens

The read is not a template with a name dropped in. Three things the user said come back verbatim:

- **What brought them here** (their pick on screen 5, or their own typed words from screen 6) appears inside the "What sets it off" section.
- **Where stress lands in their body** (their picks on question 23) is quoted in prose: "Your body's been keeping score: tight chest and restless sleep."
- **What they reach for** (their picks on question 33) is quoted in the coping section: "you reach for a drink and the endless scroll."

Plus the name, used at the age question and the promise screen.

## Screens that advance themselves
